MEDIUM BIO
Ma'Moo T. Redman is a writer and researcher based in the Midwest, raised on Chicago's South Side. His fiction explores mythology, family legacy and the stories we inherit, particularly the stories about the people who believed in things before the rest of us had proof. His nonfiction looks at the quiet, unglamorous mechanics of modern life: the habits, incentives and blind spots that shape decisions long before anyone notices they're being shaped.

He is the author of Enter Earth: Book One, a romantasy and science-fiction novel with climate-fiction and hopepunk roots that begins with an archaeological search for Noah's Ark. An expanded edition of Book One is coming soon. He has also written two self-help guides, The Manchurian Voter: Breaking the Trance and The Quiet Creep: How Remote Work Rewired You Into an Introvert, each with a companion podcast.

Two new novels are on the way. Answered in Lyrics follows a boy from a family of musicians who answers the world in the lines of songs he has memorized. Discovery of the Multiverse is a science-fiction novel about a physicist who slips into a particle collider's beam and travels through dimensions and other versions of himself.

SUGGESTED INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
1. Enter Earth begins with a search for Noah's Ark. Where did that starting point come from?
2. Enter Earth leads with romantasy and science fiction, with climate-fiction and hopepunk roots. How do those genres work together, and why hope instead of another dystopia?
3. Your fiction keeps returning to family legacy and inherited stories. What story did you inherit that shaped you most?
4. The Manchurian Voter never tells readers who to vote for. Why was it important to keep the book nonpartisan?
5. Which of the twelve signs in The Manchurian Voter did you catch in yourself first?
6. The Quiet Creep argues that remote work changed more than how we work. When did you notice it happening?
7. Both self-help books are built on ten steps and invented case studies rather than real people. Why that structure?
8. You write novels, field guides and coloring books. What connects them for you?
9. The Break started as a screenplay. What changed when you turned it into a novel?
10. How has growing up on Chicago's South Side shaped the stories you tell?
11. Why publish independently through your own imprint?
